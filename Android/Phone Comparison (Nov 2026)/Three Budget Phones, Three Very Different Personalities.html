Three Budget Phones, Three Very Different Personalities

The Infinix X6526 (Smart 8 Plus), Lava Bold N2 5G, and Samsung Galaxy M07 all sit in the affordable end of the Indian market — but they are not really competing for the same buyer. Here is how they actually stack up.

If you are shopping under ₹15,000, you have probably noticed that brands love throwing around big numbers: 6000 mAh batteries, 120Hz displays, 50MP cameras. The tricky part is figuring out which combination of specs actually matters for your daily use. The Infinix X6526 is the budget veteran, the Lava Bold N2 5G is the new 5G upstart with service convenience, and the Samsung Galaxy M07 is the software-longevity play. Let us break them down.

Infinix X6526: The "More Storage for Less" Option

The Infinix X6526 is the model number for the Infinix Smart 8 Plus, a phone that has been around long enough to see steep price cuts. Its biggest practical advantage is that it pairs 128GB of storage with 4GB of RAM at a price that often undercuts both rivals [citation:1][citation:9]. For anyone who downloads a lot of music, stores offline videos, or takes endless photos without ever touching a microSD card, that extra built-in space is genuinely useful.

The trade-offs are clear, though. The Helio G36 is an entry-level chip, the display is 90Hz (not 120Hz), and there is no 5G [citation:1][citation:9]. The software situation is also the weakest here: it ships with Android 13 Go edition, which is a stripped-down Android build meant for very low-end hardware [citation:5][citation:9]. It will work fine for WhatsApp, YouTube, and basic browsing, but do not expect a polished or long-supported experience.

Who it is for: The most price-sensitive buyer who needs maximum storage above all else and does not care about 5G or long software support.

Lava Bold N2 5G: The Battery and Service Champion

The Lava Bold N2 5G is the newest of the three, and it leans hard into two things Indian budget buyers actually care about: battery life and after-sales convenience. The 6000 mAh cell is the largest here, and Lava advertises up to 743 minutes of screen-on time [citation:2][citation:10]. The 120Hz display is also the smoothest of the trio, making scrolling and casual gaming feel noticeably more fluid than the 90Hz panels on the Infinix and Samsung [citation:2][citation:6].

The "Free Service @ Home" promise is not just marketing fluff — for buyers in smaller towns or anyone who dreads visiting a service center, having a technician come to your door for standard issues is a real differentiator [citation:10][citation:13]. The IP64 rating also gives you better dust and splash resistance than the Samsung's IP54 [citation:2][citation:3].

The compromises are in the camera and the raw chipset. The 13MP main sensor is the lowest resolution here, and the 5MP front camera is modest [citation:2][citation:6]. The Unisoc T8200 is a capable 5G chip for everyday tasks, but it is not a performance standout. Also note that while the phone supports 18W charging, the box includes only a 10W charger — you will need to buy a faster brick separately to get the quoted speeds [citation:2][citation:13].

Who it is for: The buyer who wants 5G, the longest possible battery life, a smooth 120Hz screen, and the peace of mind of at-home service — and who can live with a basic camera.

Samsung Galaxy M07: The Long-Game Software Play

The Galaxy M07 is the most "boring but sensible" phone of the three. Its headline feature is not a spec sheet number — it is the promise of 6 generations of OS upgrades and 6 years of security updates [citation:3]. No other phone in this price range comes close to that level of long-term support. If you plan to keep your phone for four or five years, this alone can justify the purchase.

The Helio G99 is also the most proven and balanced chipset here, and the phone is the slimmest and lightest at 7.6mm and 184g [citation:3][citation:4]. The 50MP main camera is a step above the Lava's 13MP sensor on paper, and the 25W fast charging is the fastest standard here (though you will need to supply your own charger, as the box does not include one) [citation:3][citation:7].

The downsides: no 5G, a 90Hz display that feels less premium than the Lava's 120Hz panel, and only 64GB of storage [citation:3][citation:4]. Samsung's One UI is feature-rich but can feel heavy on 4GB of RAM over time. And the IP54 rating is the least protective of the two rated phones here.

Who it is for: The practical buyer who values software longevity, a slim design, and a reliable camera over 5G or high refresh rates — and who is willing to buy a charger separately.

Where Each Phone Actually Wins

  • Battery life: Lava Bold N2 5G (6000 mAh). The gap is meaningful, not just on paper [citation:2][citation:10].
  • Display smoothness: Lava Bold N2 5G (120Hz vs 90Hz on both rivals) [citation:2][citation:6].
  • Raw performance & efficiency: Samsung Galaxy M07 (Helio G99 is the most well-rounded chip here) [citation:3][citation:4].
  • Software longevity: Samsung Galaxy M07 by a landslide (6 OS generations) [citation:3].
  • Storage per rupee: Infinix X6526 (128GB at the lowest entry point) [citation:1][citation:9].
  • After-sales convenience: Lava Bold N2 5G (Free Service @ Home) [citation:10][citation:13].
  • Slimness and in-hand feel: Samsung Galaxy M07 (7.6mm, 184g) [citation:3].
  • Future-proof connectivity: Lava Bold N2 5G (5G support) [citation:2][citation:10].

The short version: Choose the Lava Bold N2 5G if battery life, a smooth 120Hz screen, 5G, and at-home service matter most. Choose the Samsung Galaxy M07 if you want a phone you can keep for five-plus years with guaranteed updates and a more reliable all-round chipset. Choose the Infinix X6526 only if your absolute priority is getting 128GB of storage for the least money possible and you are okay with an older, Go-edition Android experience.

My Pick for Most Buyers: Samsung Galaxy M07

It is the least flashy phone here, but the combination of the Helio G99, a genuinely useful 50MP camera, the slimmest build, and six years of software support makes it the safest long-term purchase. The lack of 5G stings, but in the budget segment, software that stays fast and secure for years is worth more than a connectivity checkbox that many users will not fully utilize yet. If you absolutely need 5G or the biggest battery, the Lava is a strong alternative — just go in knowing the camera is basic and you will want to buy a faster charger.

A note on the Lava variant: The listing you mentioned specifies 4GB RAM + 128GB storage in Billionaire Blue. Lava's official launch materials and most retailer listings at the time of writing focus on the 64GB variant, with 128GB appearing in some spec databases at a higher price [citation:2][citation:6]. If you are buying the 128GB version, confirm the final price at checkout — it should sit above the 64GB model but still under the Samsung's typical street price.
