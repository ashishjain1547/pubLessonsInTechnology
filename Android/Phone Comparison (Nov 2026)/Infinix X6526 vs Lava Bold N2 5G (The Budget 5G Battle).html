Infinix X6526 vs Lava Bold N2 5G: The Budget 5G Battle

Two phones, two very different philosophies. One offers 5G and a massive battery, the other sticks to 4G but brings 128GB of storage. Here’s the full breakdown.

When you’re shopping in the entry-level segment, every specification matters. The Infinix X6526 (better known as the Infinix Smart 8 Plus) and the Lava Bold N2 5G occupy similar price brackets, but they target completely different priorities. The Infinix focuses on a clean 4G experience with generous storage, while Lava pushes 5G connectivity, a 120Hz display, and an enormous battery.

Let’s cut through the marketing and look at what actually matters.

Performance & Connectivity: 5G Changes Everything

This is the most consequential difference between the two phones. The Infinix X6526 runs on the MediaTek Helio G36, a 12nm chip that is strictly 4G [citation:1][citation:4]. It’s fine for WhatsApp, YouTube, and light browsing, but it will show its age as apps become heavier. The Android 13 Go Edition also limits background multitasking.

The Lava Bold N2 5G, by contrast, uses the Unisoc T8200 built on a 6nm process [citation:5]. It supports 5G networks, which means faster downloads and better longevity as carriers phase out older standards. Benchmark scores place it comfortably above the Helio G36, and the fact that it ships with Android 16 out of the box is a significant advantage for software updates and security [citation:2].

Verdict: The Lava wins this round decisively. 5G, a more efficient 6nm chip, and Android 16 make it the more future-ready device.

Display: 120Hz vs 90Hz

Both phones have HD+ resolution, which is acceptable at this price but not sharp. However, the Lava Bold N2 5G offers a 120Hz refresh rate on its 6.75-inch screen [citation:2][citation:5]. Scrolling and animations feel noticeably smoother than the 90Hz panel on the Infinix [citation:1]. The size difference is marginal (6.75 vs 6.6 inches), but the refresh rate makes the Lava feel more premium in daily use.

Winner: Lava Bold N2 5G.

Camera: Megapixels Aren’t Everything

On paper, the Infinix X6526 looks like the clear winner with a 50MP main sensor and an 8MP selfie camera [citation:1][citation:4]. The Lava counters with a 13MP rear and 5MP front .

But here’s the nuance: in budget phones, sensor quality and processing matter more than raw megapixel counts. The Infinix’s 50MP sensor does capture more detail in good light, but its low-light performance is limited by the older chipset. The Lava’s 13MP camera is more modest, but it benefits from a newer image signal processor. For social media snaps and video calls, both are adequate—but if you crop or zoom frequently, the Infinix’s higher resolution gives it a slight edge.

Winner: Infinix X6526 (on paper, and for daylight detail).

Storage: Infinix’s Secret Weapon

The base Lava Bold N2 5G typically comes with 64GB of storage, while the Infinix X6526 gives you 128GB out of the box [citation:1][citation:2]. For anyone who downloads lots of apps, stores photos locally, or plays games, that extra 64GB is invaluable. Both phones support microSD expansion, but relying on a memory card is slower and less convenient than native storage.

If you can find the 128GB Lava variant at a comparable price, this advantage disappears. But at their respective base prices, the Infinix is the storage king.

Winner: Infinix X6526.

Battery & Durability

Both phones pack a 6000 mAh battery with 18W charging support [citation:1][citation:2]. Real-world endurance will be similar—easily two days of moderate use. The Lava’s more efficient 6nm chip may give it a slight edge in screen-on time, but the difference won’t be dramatic.

The Lava Bold N2 5G pulls ahead with an IP64 rating for dust and water resistance [citation:2][citation:5]. That means it can survive splashes and dusty environments. The Infinix has no official ingress protection rating. In a country with monsoons and dusty roads, that matters.

Winner: Lava Bold N2 5G (for durability).

The “Free Service @ Home” Factor

Lava’s after-sales initiative is genuinely unique at this price point. The Free Service @ Home program sends technicians to your doorstep for basic maintenance and troubleshooting [citation:5]. For buyers in smaller towns or those who dread visiting service centers, this is a real quality-of-life feature. Infinix relies on standard service center networks, which are competent but less convenient.

Final Verdict: Which Should You Buy?

Choose Infinix X6526 if…

  • You need 128GB of storage at a low price.
  • You take a lot of daylight photos and value the 50MP sensor.
  • You don’t care about 5G and want a familiar, lightweight Android Go experience.
  • Your budget is extremely tight.

Choose Lava Bold N2 5G if…

  • You want 5G connectivity for future-proofing.
  • A 120Hz display and smoother scrolling matter to you.
  • You value IP64 durability and Android 16 out of the box.
  • Doorstep service is a deciding factor.
  • You can stretch to the 128GB variant if needed.

Overall Winner

Lava Bold N2 5G. Despite the Infinix’s storage and megapixel advantages, the Lava is the more capable and future-proof phone. 5G, a 120Hz screen, newer software, and IP64 protection are tangible benefits that you’ll notice every day. The Infinix X6526 is a solid 4G device, but in 2026, buying a 4G-only phone in this price range feels like a step backward.

Prices and availability vary by region and retailer. The Lava Bold N2 5G’s 128GB variant may not be available at all outlets; check the specific listing before purchasing.
