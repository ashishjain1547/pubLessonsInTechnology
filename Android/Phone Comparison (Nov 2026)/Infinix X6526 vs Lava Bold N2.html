Infinix X6526 vs. Lava Bold N2: The Budget Battle You Need to See

Two affordable phones, two very different philosophies. We break down the specs that actually matter.

If you’re shopping on a tight budget, the model numbers and marketing jargon can blur together fast. The Infinix X6526 (better known as the Infinix Smart 8 Plus) and the Lava Bold N2 are two names that pop up in the sub-₹10,000 conversation. But here’s the twist: the Lava listing you’re looking at—with the 5000 mAh battery and 13MP camera—is actually a different beast from the 5G version that’s been making headlines. Let’s untangle the specs and see which phone makes sense for your pocket.

Where the Infinix X6526 Pulls Ahead

The Infinix Smart 8 Plus (X6526) isn’t trying to be a flagship; it’s trying to be a reliable workhorse. And for the most part, it succeeds by playing the “more is better” card on paper.

  • Storage You Can Actually Use: 128GB of internal storage is double what the Lava offers at this tier [citation:1]. If you download a lot of WhatsApp videos or keep a few games installed, that extra space is a lifesaver. Both phones have dedicated microSD slots, but starting with 128GB means you won’t need to buy a card on day one.
  • The 50MP Camera Advantage: Megapixels aren’t everything, but a 50MP sensor generally captures more detail in daylight than a 13MP one [citation:1]. For social media snapshots, the Infinix has a clearer hardware advantage.
  • Faster Charging (Relatively): The 18W charging on the Infinix [citation:12] will top up its 6000mAh battery noticeably quicker than the Lava’s 10W brick [citation:11]. In a budget phone, every minute counts.

Where the Lava Bold N2 Fights Back

Lava has been aggressively pushing clean software and durability, and the Bold N2 4G shows that strategy in action.

  • A (Slightly) Bigger Screen: The 6.75-inch display on the Lava is marginally larger than the 6.6-inch panel on the Infinix [citation:11][citation:1]. It’s a tiny difference, but for media consumption, every fraction of an inch helps.
  • IP64 Rating: This is a tangible lifestyle feature. The Lava is officially rated for dust and water splashes [citation:11]. If you’re caught in the rain or tend to use your phone near the sink, the Lava offers peace of mind that the Infinix doesn’t advertise.
  • Newer Software Out of the Box: The Lava runs Android 15 (Go Edition) [citation:11], while the Infinix ships with Android 13 (Go Edition) [citation:4]. While both are Go versions designed for low RAM, starting two generations ahead means the Lava will likely receive security updates for a slightly longer window.

The Chipset Question: Helio G36 vs. Unisoc 9863A

Neither phone is a gaming powerhouse, but the MediaTek Helio G36 inside the Infinix [citation:1] is generally the more competent of the two. It’s built on a more modern 12nm process (versus the Lava’s 28nm chip [citation:11]), which usually translates to better power efficiency and less heat when doing basic tasks like scrolling Instagram or navigating with Google Maps. The Unisoc 9863A in the Lava is an older, budget-oriented chip that may struggle more with heavy multitasking, though Lava’s cleaner Android Go software helps mitigate that.

THE VERDICT

Choose the Infinix X6526 (Smart 8 Plus) if: You want the most hardware for your money. The 128GB storage, 50MP main camera, and larger 6000mAh battery make it a better value proposition for heavy users who prioritize photos and storage over software updates.

Choose the Lava Bold N2 (4G) if: You prioritize a cleaner, newer software experience (Android 15) and need the reassurance of an IP64 rating for dust and splash resistance. The 6.75-inch screen is also a nice bonus for watching videos.

The Catch: Availability and pricing for the 4G Lava Bold N2 can be confusing due to the overlapping 5G model. Make sure you are buying the specific 5000mAh variant if that’s what you intend to get. Meanwhile, the Infinix X6526 is a known quantity with a very competitive spec sheet for the price.
